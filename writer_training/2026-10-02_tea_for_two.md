## Было (Писатель)

Pengui keeps his mornings simple: tea, one page, a few crumbs, and no hurry. He sits in the kitchen nook with Brooklyn looking back through the window. He hums Tea for Two and taps the spoon on the saucer; the tune came from a 1920s musical, No, No, Nanette, and somehow turned into a kitchen habit.

The canary stands on top of the open cage with the air of someone who runs the place. The goldfish does a slow lap whenever something seems important. They make him not alone. When Pengui clears his throat, the canary tips its head as if to ask, well? and the fish sends up a small bubble, like a period.

He opens Simple Joys, and an old postcard slips out. Not his. City on one side; on the other, four words in a neat hand: "I was here and thought of you." It lands among the crumbs. Something loosens in his chest, the way a kettle loosens when it starts to sing. He thinks about a bench at a station and the first snow last year, and who he wanted beside him then.

Pengui turns the card over again and smiles. Near can happen at a distance, he thinks; sometimes one line on a postcard is enough. He pulls the notebook closer, chooses a name, and writes a short sentence that will fit beside the stamp. The canary approves by staying put. The goldfish offers another lap for luck.

If you like, put the kettle on and sit here for a minute. Hum a little—Tea for Two will do—and write a small postcard to someone dear. It doesn't need news. "I was here and thought of you" is already plenty.

## Стало (согласовано автором)

Pengui likes his mornings simple: tea, a biscuit, one page and no hurry. This morning, like most mornings, he hums Tea for Two and taps his spoon on the saucer in time.

The canary watches from the top of her cage with the look of someone who is, frankly, in charge here. The little red fish, her loyal support team, swishes its tail in agreement. Between the two of them, nothing in this kitchen goes unsupervised.

Then something small happened. Pengui opened a book he hadn't touched in years, and an old postcard slid out. He couldn't remember whose book it was, or how it had come to him. On the back of the card, in a neat hand: "I was here and thought of you." Somebody's little story had decided to share itself with him.

Pengui read it twice and smiled. He decided that today he would buy a postcard too, a real one, not a text, and send it to his grandmother with something very important to say: "Made your soup. It turned out well." The canary nodded, as if it had been her idea all along, and the fish swam one more lap, for luck.

Maybe you'll find a minute today, too, to send someone a couple of lines. "I'm here, and I'm thinking of you." That's enough.
